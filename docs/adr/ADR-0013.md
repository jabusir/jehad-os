# ADR-0013 — iMessage inbound gateway: paired identity, policy-gated intent

- **Status:** accepted (owner verdict 2026-09-18)
- **Date:** 2026-09-18
- **Plan:** `docs/plans/imessage-gateway.md`

## Context

E4 established iMessage as a send-only, grant-gated notification channel and
was adversarially verified with inbound closed. The owner wants iMessage as a
general conversational/control surface (mobile shell). Inbound is where
adversarial effort concentrates, so opening it requires new trust machinery
rather than weakening E4 constraints (§3 of plan; E4 review).

## Decision

1. **Paired transport identities** are the only inbound trust root. Allowlist
   entries are proven by a `josctl imessage pair` challenge (6-digit code,
   5-min TTL), never hand-configured. **Codes are single-use per transport
   handle** (first successful proof consumes the code; `--add-handle` opens
   a fresh one), throttled two-layer: per-handle lockout plus a
   session-wide total-guess cap. Identity drift fails closed to unpaired;
   the sensor cursor never contributes to identity.
2. **Intent ladder**: authenticated message = user intent, never executable
   instructions. LLM interprets into *capability proposals*; the existing
   policy layer decides execution. Two injection doors are explicitly
   bounded: inbound transport (allowlist), and tool output under the
   **authorization invariant** — tool output may influence the answer but
   can never create or expand action intent (red-teamed before Phase H).
3. **Conversation vs control modes.** Consequential verbs resolve
   deterministically via (principal, referenced object, allowed action);
   outbound review messages carry human-visible item references (`[7K4]
   approve 7K4`); bare verbs only when exactly one eligible item pends;
   ambiguity yields clarification, never action. One resolver shared with
   CLI feedback.
4. **Outbound remains exclusively the E4 send-only deliverer.** The gateway
   adds no egress path; replies are notifications to the requesting sender,
   auto-approved **only as a full conjunction** (kind=reply AND
   surface=imessage AND paired requesting principal AND recipient = that
   principal's verified transport identity AND conversation originated from
   that principal AND no third party) — never a bare kind.
5. **Identity ≠ authorization**: capabilities are granted per principal ×
   surface; iMessage surface grows phase-by-phase, each consequential
   capability an individual revocable grant.
6. **Spend bounds are principal × surface** (requests/hour, tokens/day,
   cost/day) enforced at `callModel` reservation; gateway max loss is
   dollar-bounded independent of other surfaces. **[ADR-0007]**
7. **Phasing A′→A→B→C committed** (A′ de-risks current-macOS chat.db
   access: FDA under launchd identity, WAL/immutable read-only mode,
   `text`+`attributedBody` decoding, loud failure on drift); D–H are
   sequenced intent. Phase A exits on a 48h lifecycle/event matrix (not
   wall-clock), with loop defense (`is_from_me` +
   `sent_message_fingerprints`) validated empirically before any LLM
   exists; `interaction_threads` and `interaction_messages` land at Phase D.

## Consequences

- iMessage becomes the first non-CLI interaction surface; `interaction_threads`
  (Phase D) is surface-agnostic so web/voice/CLI reuse it.
- The sensor (chat.db, FDA, schema drift) is the highest-uncertainty
  component and sits on the critical path — hence the A′ spike.
- Group chats, attachments, SMS fallback are explicitly out of scope
  (plan §10).
